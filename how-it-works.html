How it works.

An election in COLection has five steps, always in the same order. Each step tells you when it is ready.

The five steps.

1

Set the rules

Say who is being elected and how: single roles, committees, limits on groups, and what happens in a tie. Start from a ready-made template or build your own.

2

Collect the votes

Import answers from Google Forms, run the vote in the room on phones and voting stations, or send every voter a personal link so they can vote from anywhere. You can do the last two at the same time.

3

Check

Before anything is counted, COLection checks that the rules and the people standing fit together, and tells you plainly if they do not.

4

Count and read the results

Count, settle any ties, then read the winners and the round-by-round tallies.

5

Export a report

Choose what goes in the printed report and watch the PDF update as you tick things. Then save it, print it or sign it off.

Voting on the day.

One computer is the host. Voters join from a QR code on their phone, or sit at a voting station that goes full screen and locks until a password is entered.

On a phone or at a station

A person at the desk checks who each voter is and sends them a ballot. A phone voter scans a QR code, with nothing to install. A station voter votes on a computer in the room.

  • People who cannot come. Start online voting and every voter gets a personal link, by email or by hand. Someone who votes at a station can never also use their link.
  • No voter list? Register people at the desk as they arrive. At the station they type their ID again before they vote. Only people who vote are kept.
  • Several desks. More computers can join the host as extra desks, so more than one person can register voters at once.
  • If the host fails. Stations keep finished ballots, and a second computer can take over from a backup so no vote is lost.
  • Names stay off ballots. A ballot carries no name and no time, and new ballots are placed at a random position.

Templates.

Start from a template, keep your own

The library has ready-made setups for university unions, residential colleges, schools, clubs, community groups, awards nights and yes or no motions. Save your own rules as a template for next year, or to share. Only the rules are saved, never candidates, voters or votes. Templates are starting points: check them against your own constitution.
